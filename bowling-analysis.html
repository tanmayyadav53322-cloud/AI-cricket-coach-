Bowling Action Analysis

Bowling analysis covers both fast bowling and spin bowling, looking at the parts of a bowling action that are visible on video.

Fast bowling

  • Run-up rhythm — consistency of approach into the crease.
  • Gather — the transition from run-up into the delivery stride.
  • Delivery stride and front-leg position — how the front leg lands and braces.
  • Shoulder and hip alignment — how side-on or front-on the body is at landing.
  • Arm action and release position — when visible on camera.
  • Follow-through and balance — the finish of the action.

Spin bowling

  • Approach and balance — a typically shorter, more controlled run-up than pace bowling.
  • Delivery stride and body rotation.
  • Release position, when the camera angle shows it.
  • Follow-through.

Wrist and finger position at release are part of what shapes spin, but they're often too small or too fast to judge reliably from a typical phone video — where the evidence doesn't support a confident observation, the platform is built to say so rather than guess.

Improving your bowling action

Coaches commonly recommend building a repeatable run-up and delivery stride before chasing extra pace or turn, and filming from a consistent side-on angle to track change over time. See cricket training drills for run-up rhythm, alignment, and release drills.

Frequently asked questions

How can I improve my bowling action?

Consistency usually comes before pace or turn: repeating the same run-up rhythm, a stable delivery stride, and a repeatable release point. Filming your action from the side and comparing it over time — alongside our bowling drills — is a practical way to track changes.

How can I improve cricket footwork?

For bowlers, footwork mainly means run-up rhythm and a stable front-leg landing at the crease. For batters, it means moving early and decisively to get into position for front-foot or back-foot shots. Both benefit from slow, repetition-based practice before adding pace or match intensity.

Does the platform diagnose bowling injuries or biomechanical risk?

No. AI Cricket Coach does not diagnose injuries or assess biomechanical injury risk. It can note visibly observable technique patterns, but any pain, discomfort or suspected injury should be assessed by a qualified coach or a medical professional.
