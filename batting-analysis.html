Batting Technique Analysis

Batting analysis on AI Cricket Coach looks at the parts of a batting shot a video can actually show, organized the way a coach might talk through an innings on film.

What batting analysis looks at

  • Stance and balance — how a player sets up before the ball is bowled.
  • Head position — how still and level the head stays through the shot; many coaches treat a stable head as one contributor to consistent contact.
  • Footwork — front-foot and back-foot movement in response to the ball.
  • Weight transfer — how weight shifts from the back foot to the front foot (or stays back) during the shot.
  • Bat path — the swing of the bat, and contact position when the camera angle shows it.
  • Follow-through — the finish of the shot and balance afterward.

What is a good batting stance?

A widely taught baseline is a balanced, side-on stance: weight spread evenly, knees softly flexed, head still and roughly level over the front knee, and the bat held comfortably near the back toe. This is a starting template rather than a fixed rule — plenty of successful batters adapt it, and a coach can help judge what suits an individual player's height, style and the format they're playing.

How to improve batting technique

Common, well-established approaches include practicing the fundamentals in isolation (shadow batting in front of a mirror), facing throwdowns or a side-arm thrower to repeat a specific shot, and reviewing your own footage regularly to see what's changed. Frequently asked questions

How can I improve my batting technique?

Most improvement comes from repetition with feedback: practice your fundamentals (stance, head position, footwork) through shadow batting and throwdowns, film yourself regularly, and compare footage over time. See our batting drills for specific practice ideas.

What is a good batting stance?

A commonly taught starting point is a balanced, side-on stance with weight spread evenly between both feet, knees softly flexed, head still and roughly level, and the bat resting comfortably near the back toe. Coaches vary in the exact details, and many top players adapt this base to suit their own game.

What does batting video analysis measure?

Visible elements only: stance and balance, head position, footwork, weight transfer, bat path, contact position when the camera shows it, and follow-through. See AI cricket video analysis for what it can and can't measure.
