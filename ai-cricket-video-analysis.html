AI Cricket Video Analysis, Explained

AI cricket video analysis is the practice of using computer vision to look at a player's own recorded video and describe what's visibly happening in cricket-coaching terms — rather than relying on someone's memory of how a shot or delivery felt.

What is pose estimation?

Pose estimation is a general computer-vision technique that locates key points on a person's body — shoulders, hips, knees, wrists — in each frame of a video, effectively sketching a stick-figure of their posture over time. It isn't specific to cricket; the same underlying technology is used in sports analysis, fitness apps and animation. AI Cricket Coach applies it to cricket-specific movement, such as a batting stance or a bowling action.

What can be measured from a video — and what can't

Because the input is a single 2D video, movement analysis has real limits. It's well suited to visible, observable things:

  • Batting stance, balance and head position
  • Footwork — front-foot and back-foot movement, weight transfer
  • Bat path and follow-through
  • Bowling run-up rhythm, delivery stride, arm action and release position when visible

It is not suited to measurements a camera alone can't capture reliably: exact ball speed or spin revolutions, internal joint stress or injury risk, or anything the camera angle or lighting hides. AI Cricket Coach is built to say "unable to assess reliably from this video" rather than invent an answer when evidence is insufficient.

Why confidence levels matter

Every possible technique issue the platform reports is meant to carry a confidence level — High, Medium or Low — reflecting how clearly the video supports that observation. A low-confidence note usually means the camera angle or video quality left room for doubt, and it's flagged as such rather than presented as certain.

Frequently asked questions

What is AI cricket video analysis?

AI cricket video analysis is the use of computer vision — specifically pose estimation — to observe a player's body position and movement in a video, and relate what's visible to established cricket coaching concepts like stance, footwork, head position, or bowling arm action.

What does batting video analysis measure?

It looks at what a camera can actually show: stance and balance, head position, front-foot and back-foot movement, weight transfer, bat path, contact position when visible, and follow-through. It does not measure things a single video can't capture, such as bat speed at the point of contact or internal joint loading.

Can AI replace a real cricket coach?

No. AI Cricket Coach is designed to support practice between coaching sessions and give players a structured way to review their own video — not to replace a qualified coach's judgment, in-person feedback, or a medical professional's advice on pain or injury.
