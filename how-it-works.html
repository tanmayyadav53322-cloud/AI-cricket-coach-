How AI Cricket Coach Works

AI Cricket Coach is ai-powered cricket video analysis and training platform. It turns a phone-recorded batting or bowling video into structured, cricket-specific feedback and a training plan, following a clear pipeline from upload to practice.

The five-step pipeline

  1. 1. Upload or record

    Choose a video from your gallery or record one with your device camera. You pick what you're working on — Batting, Fast Bowling, Spin Bowling, Wicketkeeping or Fielding — and can note what you're trying to improve.

  2. 2. Validate

    Before anything is analyzed, the app checks duration (3–90 seconds), file size (under 200 MB), resolution and lighting. This step is live today and runs entirely from your device.

  3. 3. Analyze

    A pose-estimation model — a computer-vision technique that locates body joints in each video frame — is used to observe visible movement: stance, footwork, head position, bat path or bowling arm action, depending on the analysis type. Read how AI cricket video analysis works.

  4. 4. Feedback

    Observations are organized into strengths, possible technique issues (each with a confidence level), and things the video couldn't show clearly enough to judge.

  5. 5. Train

    The platform suggests relevant drills and can build a 7-day training plan around the areas identified, alongside a general training library anyone can use.

What's real today, and what's in progress

Steps 1 and 2 — upload, recording and video validation — are fully functional. Step 3, the AI video-analysis engine itself, is under active development: until a computer-vision service is connected, the app is explicit that analysis isn't available yet rather than showing invented feedback. Frequently asked questions

How does cricket video analysis work?

A player uploads or records a short batting or bowling video. The app checks that the video is long enough, clear enough and well-lit, then (once a computer-vision service is connected) a pose-estimation model locates the body's joints in each frame so the platform can describe visible movement — such as head position, footwork or arm action — in cricket coaching terms.

Is the analysis automatic, or does a coach review it?

The analysis itself is automated: it comes from a computer-vision model looking at your video, not from a human coach watching it live. AI Cricket Coach is built to support a player's own practice and a coach's existing input, not to replace either.

What video length and quality does the app need?

Videos should be between 3 and 90 seconds long, under 200 MB, at least 480p resolution, and well lit, with as much of the player's body visible as possible. The app checks these automatically before analysis and explains what to fix if a video doesn't meet them.
